# Step 2 — Dataset Selection and Feasibility Assessment

**Decision:** Select **UCI Online Retail II**, using both worksheets in the original `online_retail_II.xlsx` workbook.

**Assessment date:** 2026-09-21. **Status:** Selection complete. This historical intake report precedes the completed audit and cleaning; use the final case study for business results.

The workbook was downloaded from the original UCI distribution, fingerprinted, and profiled in full. The selection is based on actual customer and invoice coverage as well as source documentation. Screening results below are provisional intake statistics, not final business findings.

## Why this dataset

The project needs identifiable customers, distinct purchase occasions, monetary fields, and enough follow-up to compare cohorts. Online Retail II supports all four and provides substantial repeat-purchase histories. Its two-year coverage offers more mature cohorts than the shorter Online Retail release.

The analytical case will use historical UK retail data. The Vietnam marketplace scenario remains a motivation for the skills being demonstrated; numerical findings will describe this source retailer only. The project will not rename countries, convert currency to imply Vietnamese transactions, or manufacture missing marketplace fields.

## Alternatives considered

| Candidate | Evidence reviewed | Strengths for this project | Limitations and decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UCI Online Retail II** | Original UCI page, license, downloaded workbook, complete feasibility profile | Approximately two years of invoice-level purchase history; identifiable repeat customers; quantities and prices; open attribution license | Historical UK gift retail with wholesale customers; no native categories, promotions, delivery, or payment fields. **Selected for its customer-history depth.** |
| **UCI Online Retail** | Original UCI dataset documentation; workbook not separately profiled | Similar invoice, customer, date, and monetary fields; smaller download | Approximately one year of coverage gives less history and follow-up. **Not selected because Online Retail II provides a longer window.** Do not append this release to Online Retail II as additional independent transactions. |
| **Olist Brazilian E-Commerce** | Publisher's Kaggle data card and customer-table documentation; files not downloaded or profiled | Rich multi-table marketplace structure, categories, geography, delivery and payments | Customer histories must use `customer_unique_id`, not the order-specific `customer_id`; follow-up and repeat-purchase density would require a separate audit. Published license is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. **Not selected for this project; stronger operational detail does not replace demonstrated customer-history suitability.** |
| **Custom synthetic Vietnam marketplace** | Design option, not an acquired dataset | Could include desired local geography and promotion fields | Repeat-purchase patterns would reflect generator assumptions. **Not selected as the primary evidence base.** |

This is a fit assessment for this project, not a general ranking of datasets. Only the selected workbook received a full file-level feasibility profile; no unverified Olist retention rate is claimed.

## Repeat-purchase feasibility

For this screen only, a candidate purchase line has a customer ID and invoice, a valid date, positive quantity and price, and an invoice that does not start with `C`. Distinct invoices, rather than line items, identify candidate purchase occasions.

| Preliminary measure | Count |
|---|---:|
| Candidate purchase lines | 805,549 |
| Customers with candidate purchases | 5,878 |
| Distinct candidate invoices | 36,969 |
| Customers with more than one distinct candidate invoice | 4,255 |
| Customers with at least 30 days of potential follow-up | 5,717 |
| Customers with at least 60 days of potential follow-up | 5,518 |
| Customers with at least 90 days of potential follow-up | 5,281 |
| Customers with at least 180 days of potential follow-up | 4,977 |

Potential follow-up is measured from the first observed candidate purchase to the last candidate timestamp. These counts show that fixed-window analysis is possible. They are not repeat-purchase rates or final eligible sample sizes: special item codes, adjustment records, cancellations, coverage assumptions, and final invoice validity still need review. Customer IDs have no external identity validation; they are source identifiers, not verified individual people.

## Issues that must carry forward

| Observed issue or source limitation | Evidence | Required treatment in later steps |
|---|---|---|
| Overlapping worksheets | The first sheet ends on 2010-12-09; the second begins on 2010-12-01. In the second sheet, 22,523 rows match at least one decoded row in the first sheet. | Retain sheet and source-row lineage during ingestion. Resolve overlap before summing transactions or revenue. |
| Repeated source rows | 34,335 repeated rows after the first occurrence across the workbook, comparing all eight decoded fields | Investigate overlap and within-sheet repetition separately; identical lines can require business judgment. Do not blindly sum the overlap and within-sheet counts, which overlap with each other. |
| Missing customer identity | 243,007 rows lack `Customer ID` | Exclude unidentified buyers from customer-linked retention. Decide separately whether their valid transactions belong in overall sales totals; explain the denominator difference. |
| Cancellations and adjustments | 19,494 rows have a `C` invoice prefix; 22,950 rows have nonpositive quantities; 6,207 have nonpositive prices | Audit their meanings and define sale/return/adjustment treatment. These flag counts are not mutually exclusive. |
| Missing descriptions | 4,382 rows lack a nonblank description | Audit product-code mappings; do not invent categories from incomplete names without a separate labeled methodology. |
| Incomplete final month | Data ends during December 2011 | Treat December 2011 as a partial month. Monthly cohort tables need complete observation months; daily-window analysis needs an explicit cutoff. |
| Left-censored customer histories | Transactions before December 2009 are unavailable | Use “first observed purchase” terminology; early cohorts may include existing customers. Consider an initial history period or sensitivity analysis in Step 3. |
| Historical retailer and wholesale mix | Source context differs from a modern Vietnam marketplace | Limit generalization and inspect unusually large baskets; do not assume all accounts are ordinary retail consumers or classify wholesale buyers without evidence. |
| Limited service and margin information | No native delivery, payment, discount, category, cost, or acquisition-channel fields | Restrict claims and label monetary measures precisely. Do not infer profit, promotional effects, or full net revenue from quantity times price alone. |

The intake parser found no invalid date, quantity, or price values and no invoice associated with multiple nonmissing customer IDs. These narrow checks do not establish that every invoice is valid or that the data is fully clean.

## Final supported scope

| Proposed analysis | Decision after dataset review |
|---|---|
| Monthly orders, sales value, purchasing customers, and average order value | Supported after transaction rules are defined |
| New versus returning customers | Supported using first **observed** purchase, with history limitations stated |
| 30/60/90-day repeat purchasing | Supported with eligibility windows; 180 days is feasible as an optional extension |
| Monthly cohort retention | Supported with overlap resolution and incomplete-month handling |
| RFM and revenue concentration | Supported; scores must handle tied and skewed values |
| Historical customer value and inactivity-based reactivation groups | Supported as descriptive analysis |
| Geographic comparisons | Country only, subject to adequate customer counts; no city/district claims |
| First-purchase differences | Use first-order basket value, item diversity, or validated product-code groupings; define these in Step 3 |
| Discount, acquisition-channel, native category, delivery, and payment analysis | Excluded from the primary scope because the fields are absent |
| Predictive CLV, confirmed churn, and measured campaign impact | Outside the initial project scope |

## Acquisition and reproducibility

See [`data/raw/README.md`](../data/raw/README.md) for download options, attribution, and checksum verification. The GitHub upload folder excludes the workbook; restore the verified source with the download command below. `.gitignore` keeps it out of ordinary Git commits; the source manifest and access instructions are tracked instead.

From the repository root, with Python 3.10 or later:

```bash
python src/download_dataset.py
python src/profile_dataset.py
```

Both scripts use only the Python standard library. The first verifies the existing file or downloads the pinned workbook. The second reads both sheets without changing them and recreates the JSON feasibility profile. The date reader assumes the verified Excel 1900 date system and these eight source columns; it is not a general Excel reader.
